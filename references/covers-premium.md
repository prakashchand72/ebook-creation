# Covers — Premium Direction

The bare technical specs (pixel dimensions, DPI, bleed, file format) live in the `ebook-publishing` skill's `cover-specs.md`. **This file is about composition** — what makes a cover read as expensive.

## The thumbnail test

The cover will be seen at 200 × 300 pixels in 95% of buying decisions. **Design at the thumbnail size first.** If it doesn't work small, it doesn't work.

Open your design at 200px wide on a phone screen. If you can't read the title, can't tell the genre, and don't feel anything in two seconds — start over.

## The five archetypes that always work

Most premium covers fall into one of these. Pick consciously.

### 1. Type-only

No image. Just type, set deliberately. Risky in the wrong hands; effortless prestige in the right ones.
- Examples: most Penguin Modern Classics, Coddiwomple, Rework, Show Your Work.
- Works for: business, ideas, memoir, essays, manifestos.
- Required: at least one striking typographic move (oversized title, color block, grid play, custom letterform).

### 2. Single object on negative space

One photographed or illustrated object, centered or off-center, on a flat color field. Title and author small.
- Examples: most New Yorker book covers, the Picador classics series.
- Works for: literary fiction, narrative non-fiction.
- Required: the object must *mean* something — it's an image from inside the book, not a stock photo of a vaguely related thing.

### 3. Full-bleed photograph with type overlay

A photograph fills the cover; the type sits on it with high contrast (often a translucent color block behind the title).
- Examples: most travel writing, food memoirs, place-based non-fiction.
- Works for: lifestyle, travel, food, photography books.
- Required: the photograph is *yours* (or licensed editorial), not stock. Stock photos read as cheap because everyone has seen them.

### 4. Custom illustration

A commissioned illustration — figurative, abstract, or somewhere between.
- Examples: most Penguin Press hardcovers, anything illustrated by Olly Moss / Coralie Bickford-Smith.
- Works for: any genre if the illustration is genuinely original.
- Required: a commissioned artist or genuinely unique generated illustration. Generic "AI cover" looks signal cheapness now.

### 5. Conceptual / graphic

A bold graphic device — a shape, a pattern, a die-cut feeling — that becomes the visual signature.
- Examples: anything by Chip Kidd, Influence by Cialdini, Sapiens (UK edition).
- Works for: ideas books, business, anything with a single core concept.
- Required: a single, sharp idea. Don't combine archetypes.

## The cheap-cover tells

Avoid these — they are the giveaway signs of self-published work:

- **Three or more typefaces on the cover.** Pick one (or two if a sans/serif pair).
- **Drop shadow on the title.** Always reads as amateur.
- **Bevel and emboss.** Same.
- **A photograph of a person looking at the camera.** Almost always reads as stock. Crop to a hand, an object, a fragment.
- **Author name larger than the title** (unless the author is a brand, e.g., Stephen King — then it's correct).
- **Stock illustration of a lightbulb / brain / chess piece / arrow** for a business book. Done to death.
- **A "5-star bestseller" badge** added in design. If the book is a bestseller it goes on the back, not the front; if it's not, don't lie.
- **Centered everything.** A premium cover usually has at least one strong off-center element.
- **Default Adobe Stock gradients.** They have a look. Don't.

## Composition rules

- **Establish a focal point.** One thing the eye lands on first. Title, image, color block — but only one.
- **Three zones, not five.** Title, author, and a single visual element. Subtitle if essential. Anything else (publisher, blurbs, "from the author of") goes on the back of a print book; on an ebook, omit.
- **Asymmetry is more memorable than symmetry.** Hang the title left or right of center.
- **Contrast is non-negotiable.** Title against background must pass 7:1 contrast at minimum (higher than body-text WCAG, because the cover must work at thumbnail).
- **Bleed safely.** Anything within 6mm of the edge will be cropped on print runs. For ebook-only, edge-to-edge is fine.

## Type on covers

- Use a **display face** for the title — something with character (not Helvetica). Strong serifs (Caslon, Knockout, Tiempos Headline), heavy condensed sans (Druk, Druk Wide, GT Walsheim), or a true display face (Kepler Display, Editorial New).
- The body face from the interior can come back on the cover for the author name and subtitle. This creates a connection between the cover and the inside.
- **Track tightly** for premium feel. `letter-spacing: -0.02em` for serif display, `-0.01em` for sans display. Never set a display title at default tracking — it looks word-processed.
- **Stack and break the title intentionally.** Long titles should break on a meaningful word: "How to Build / a Premium / Brand", not "How to Build a / Premium Brand".

## Genre rules

Reader expectations are real. A premium romance cover, a premium thriller cover, and a premium business cover are *different* — and those differences are not negotiable. Know the genre before designing.

- **Business / ideas**: type-driven, color-blocked, restrained.
- **Literary fiction**: object on negative space, illustrated, often hand-lettered title.
- **Thriller / mystery**: high contrast, single graphic motif, often dark.
- **Romance**: figure or object, warm palette, often hand-script for the title.
- **Memoir**: photographic, often a fragment of a personal image.
- **Self-help**: type-driven if premium, photographic-with-author if mass-market.
- **Cookbooks / lifestyle**: photographic, often with the title set in a confident sans.

Don't fight the genre. Premium does not mean abstract — it means executed at the highest level *within* the conventions of the category.

## Spine, back, thumbnail

Even for ebooks:

- **Thumbnail (Amazon, Apple Books)**: the cover at 160 × 240px. This is the actual sale-driving asset. Test legibility here first.
- **Spine** (print): often forgotten. Title, author, publisher mark. Set vertically (read top to bottom). Use the same display face as the cover.
- **Back** (print): one-paragraph hook (NOT a synopsis), one or two blurbs, author bio thumbnail, ISBN/barcode. Set in the body face from the interior. The back is composed, not a wall of text.

## The cover brief — what to ask the user before designing

1. **One sentence**: what is this book about?
2. **One adjective**: how should the cover *feel* (calm / urgent / mysterious / joyful / serious / playful)?
3. **One reference cover**: a real book cover the user thinks "feels right" — and one they explicitly *don't* want to look like.
4. **Genre**: confirm the category for shelving and reader expectations.
5. **Format**: ebook only, ebook + print, hardcover with dust jacket?
6. **Author profile**: is this a debut or an established author? (Established → name larger.)

Don't move past these answers. Premium cover work is impossible without them.
